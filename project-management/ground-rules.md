# Ground Rules

## Team Procedures

Team meeting times; communication mediums and frequency (email alone is not enough); decision-making; meeting agendas; record keeping; and your branching structure, naming conventions, and branch ownership within the [GitHub Process](../technical-documents/github-process.md), including whether you will use one `project-management` branch or one per iteration part for your [project management updates](../technical-documents/github-process.md#project-management-branches), where REST API design changes will go, and how you will organize the system tests so feature branches can update them without colliding (see [Technical Document Branches](../technical-documents/github-process.md#technical-document-branches)).

- Sunday synchronous meetings at 2pm if needed

Branching:
-    one project management/documentation branch
-    naming conventions: unityId/uc#_feature-based-role
-    feature-based role branches: backend, frontend-entry, frontend-report

## Work Quality

Your standards of quality for code, tests, documentation, bug reports, and other deliverables; how you will ensure quality (pair programming, inspection, peer review, ...); and how you will follow the required processes, plus any processes of your own.

- Make sure coverage meets done criteria
- Make sure everything's documented
- Tag issues in commit messages
- Review code by someone other than requester in the pull requests
- Mention any known bugs in the commits/pull request description

## Team Participation

Cooperation and equal distribution of tasks, inclusiveness, keeping on task, leadership and roles.

- Split up tasks evenly every week according to feature based roles.
- Complete work by synchronous team meeting or update team on status by synchronous meeting

## Accountability

The level of attendance, participation, responsibility, communication, and commitment expected of each team member, and what a member should do if they can't fulfill their commitments for a week or longer.

- Everyone is expected to attend team meetings or let team know in advance if they cannot make it.
- If you cannot complete your work one week, expected to be given something a little more difficult next week.
- If one week someone's task took more time than others, work will be distributed to make up the difference the next week.

## Consequences for Non-Participation

How the team will handle a first incident of non-participation, repeated incidents, and extended non-participation.  The teaching staff will use this section as a basis for grade modifications if they become necessary.

- If someone doesn't show up for meetings consistently and doesn't complete their work consistently, we will reach out to the teaching staff.
- We will also complete the peer evaluation form.
